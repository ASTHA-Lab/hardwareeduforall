Hardware Security Education for All

A collaborative curriculum project. The modules were built so that a department can teach hardware security without standing up a dedicated course, and so that the students who never elect that course still meet the ideas.

The paper this site follows

The structure of the site is the structure of the paper Bringing Hardware Security Education to All: A Seamless Curriculum Integration Approach, prepared for ISQED 2027. The paper states the three obstacles, the five design rules, the nine-module catalog, the deployment record, and the limits of the evidence. Where a lab manual and the paper disagree on a detail of procedure, the manual is what a student follows, and the paper is what this site uses for status, scope, and assessment.

The workforce context in the paper is worth keeping next to the curriculum claim. An industry analysis projects growth in the U.S. semiconductor workforce from about 345,000 to about 460,000 positions by 2030, with a large share of new technical roles at risk of going unfilled. Employment of information security analysts is projected to grow much faster than employment overall. The graduates who can work across hardware and security come from the same programs whose required courses still skip the security of the hardware.

What a department can take

  • The selection principle and the five rules, if you want to write a module of your own.
  • The catalog, if you want to drop an existing module into a course you already teach.
  • The evidence page, if you need to know what has been tried and what is still only written down.
