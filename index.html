A separate course does not reach the students who need this.

Limited reach

The elective is taken by a few.

At the University of Florida, fewer than 7% of undergraduate electrical and computer engineering students register for the introductory hardware security course in a given year. Students who never planned a security career do not see the material, and the students missing from that room are missing from the workforce that follows.

Cost

A new course is expensive to keep.

A dedicated course needs an instructor who already knows the subject, a lab of special equipment, and a slot in a crowded curriculum. Teaching-focused departments compete for a hire they are unlikely to make. A course taken by a small fraction of the majors is hard to justify.

Fragmented view

The attack gets separated from the design choice.

Many of these failures start in implementation. A side channel is a property of how a gate switches, not a story told later in a security elective. A student who meets the attack apart from the circuit learns a fragment.

A security concept earns its place when it gives the student a reason to use a design concept the course already has to teach. Performing the attack is how the student works the design topic.

The selection principle

Nine modules

Three have survey data from embedded systems. Two more have been taught. Four are written and ready to run. Pick at most three in a term.
