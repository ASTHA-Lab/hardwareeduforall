Cache side channel

Treat the cache the course already teaches as a channel another process can read.

The course has already taught the cache as a performance structure. This module teaches the same structure as a channel. A hit and a miss on a shared last-level line take different numbers of cycles, and the gap is wide enough to read another process.

Outcome

The lecture closes on a recorded probe-time series. Across 90 probes, hits fall between 96 and 104 cycles and misses fall between 144 and 168. A threshold at 110 sits in a gap of about 40 cycles. No graded assignment is stated in the materials, and no survey was given.

Part 1. The cache as the course already draws it

Hierarchy from registers to main memory, the parameters that define a cache, and the three associativity arrangements. Students read the sizes, associativity, and line size of their own machine from the operating system and from a topology tool.

Part 2. The same structure as a channel

A flush-and-reload attack needs four things: memory shared with the victim, a high-resolution cycle counter, an instruction that evicts a chosen line, and a last-level cache shared across cores. The lecture walks the sequence. Flush a shared line, let the victim run, reload the line and time the access. The flush is the cache-line flush intrinsic with fences on both sides. The reload is timed between two reads of the timestamp counter. When no memory is shared, the lecture turns to prime-and-probe, which fills a set and times the later access to see whether the victim evicted a line.

Fall 2024 is the offering in which this lecture entered the computer architecture course. The slide decks below are the two parts.
