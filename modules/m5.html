Power side channel

The topic

Students watch the supply current of a gate. A spike shows that the output changed. It does not show whether the output went high or low.

Where it belongs

Course: Digital logic.
Teach this after: CMOS switching and truth tables. An earlier version was homework in embedded systems in Spring 2022.

  • Mangard, Oswald, and Popp, Power Analysis Attacks: Revealing the Secrets of Smart Cards, Springer, 2007. The book-length treatment. This lab is one gate and one sense resistor.

The exercise

An unknown two-input gate sits in a box. The attacker can see inputs A and B and the supply pin. The output is hidden. A resistor between the supply pin and the source turns switching current into a voltage. A large change means the output changed. A small bump does not. The direction of the change is not in the trace.

Outcome

The transient reports that an output transition happened and not which way it went. A gate and its complement produce the same transition table. The student names both candidates and explains why the measurement cannot separate them. The module asks for no defense. That ambiguity is the entrance to masking.

The work

  1. Open the unknown circuit and step the inputs. Capture the voltage plot for the sequence 00, then 01, then 11, and mark the moments the inputs change.
  2. For each row of the transition table, stop the simulation, reset, apply the initial input, run about 20 ms, change to the new input, and run about 20 ms. Record whether a real transition occurred.
  3. From that table, give every truth table that would produce the same transitions, and show the work.

The early form of this exercise was a graded homework in embedded systems in Spring 2022, before the module moved to the digital-logic setting the catalog now uses. No survey was collected for it.
